About

I’m a computer scientist from Chicago, most at home where math meets systems.

I’m finishing my B.S. in computer science at Syracuse University, graduating in May 2027, and continuing at the University of Chicago as an Advanced Scholar in the M.S. in Computer Science program, with a focus on high-performance computing.

I also teach. I’m a teaching assistant for Discrete Mathematics and facilitate weekly workshops for Intro to Computer Science. Away from the keyboard, I play guitar, compete semi-professionally in Fortnite: Battle Royale, and run.

Research and projects

Optimal market making under inventory risk

Personal project, Aug 2026 – present

An implementation of the Avellaneda–Stoikov model, which frames quote placement as a stochastic optimal control problem: capture the spread, but shift an inventory-adjusted reservation price to limit the risk of holding too much.

The simulator keeps its performance-critical core in C++ and exposes it to a Python research layer through pybind11, mirroring the compiled-core and research-layer split of production quant systems.

Python, C++, pybind11, NumPy, pandas, CMake

Scalable matrix approximation

Undergraduate research, Syracuse University, Jan – May 2026

I designed gradient-based methods that approximate large matrices with tridiagonal Toeplitz matrices, which are far cheaper to store and compute with, while preserving the spectral properties that matter.

To keep query costs low on large datasets, I built stochastic gradient descent algorithms around randomized matrix-vector products and trace estimators.

Undergraduate research with Prof. Venkata Gandikota, Syracuse University, Jan – May 2026

Syracuse University shuttle network analysis

SOURCE undergraduate research, Syracuse University, Jan – Apr 2025

In a team of four, I modeled the campus shuttle system as a graph in Python with NetworkX: 36 stops connected by more than 40 unique routes.

On that model I built route-optimization software using Dijkstra’s shortest-path algorithm, the same one running in Figure 1. It pinpointed bottlenecks in the network and found rerouting options with potential time savings of up to 27%.

Python, NetworkX
